## Reflection 

Throughout this project, our initial ideas have developed into a more focused comparison of public transport connectivity in Kreuzberg and Wilmersdorf during the division of Berlin. One of our most important decisions was selecting the years and distance thresholds for our analysis, as these choices influenced how we interpreted accessibility. We learned that our dataset provides valuable information about the transport network but cannot directly explain people's travel behaviours and experiences. This revealed uncertainties and potential biases, particularly the assumption that better connectivity necessarily meant better accessibility for residents.

In terms of digital methods, we learned how coding and visualisations can help us identify patterns in historical data. However, time constraints limited us to relatively basic visualisations and we could not conduct more advanced network analysis. Our workflow generally worked well because we divided tasks according to our academic strengths and made decisions altogether. Nevertheless, miscommunication between our research question and thesis statement required revision and temporarily slowed our progress.

Looking back, there are several aspects of our project that we would approach differently. We would spend more time developing a clear research question and thesis statement at the beginning of the project. During our collaboration, we had some miscommunication because our RQ and the thesis statement did not fully align, which temporarily slowed our progress. Establishing a clearer direction earlier would have helped us connect our historical research, data analysis, and visualisations more effectively from the start.

Also, we would try to incorporate more human perspectives into our analysis. Although our dataset allows us to examine the development and spatial distribution of Berlin's public transport network, it does not tell us how residents actually experienced these changes in their everyday lives. By including more historical testimonies, or archival sources, we could better understand how transport accessibility related to people's daily routines of living in a divided city. We would also like to conduct more advanced network analysis and develop an interactive map or website that allows users to explore the historical transport network themselves.
