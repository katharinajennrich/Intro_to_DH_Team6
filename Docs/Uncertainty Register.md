## Uncertainty Register
Our project encountered several uncertainties that may influence the interpretation of our findings. One significant uncertainty concerns potentially missing or incomplete station records in the historical public transport dataset. Since the dataset contains transcribed timetable data from selected years between 1946 and 1989, missing records or transcription errors may affect the accuracy of our station coverage calculations and comparisons between Kreuzberg and Wilmersdorf. We classify this as data-related and historical uncertainty. To manage it, we based our analysis on the available records and avoided assuming that the dataset provides a complete representation of the historical transport network. This uncertainty remains unresolved because we cannot independently verify every record.

Another potential source of bias is language bias. Our reliance on English sources may exclude relevant German historical documents, local perspectives, and interpretations of Berlin's division. This may influence how we understand the historical context and explain differences between the two districts. We acknowledge this limitation by distinguishing evident findings from our own interpretations and avoiding unsupported causal claims. These uncertainties limit the extent to which our results can represent the complete historical situation. Therefore, we communicate our findings as interpretations of the available data rather than definitive conclusions about how Berlin's division shaped public transport accessibility.
