## Bias and Positionality Statement
As written in the Limitation Statement, the outcome produced by our project was not only affected by the limitations of the dataset itself, but also decided by our prospective and modelling choices. The dataset mainly described transport networks and information recorded by the official government, which left not many spaces and situations for normal academic biases such as prejudices upon specific social groups or cultural background. We can see quantifiable elements such as stations, lines, frequencies (refers to the time intervals between two buses on this route at 7:30 AM on weekdays), longitude and latitude clearly in data information, while a lot of information over social background is fully invisible, thus we cannot determine whether changes of connectivity produced influences upon economy or politics. Based on that, we narrowed our research scope to merely the connectivity of two administrative districts in West Berlin. We took the concept “connectivity” from Misha’s research on multidimensional understanding of connectivity while our project mainly focused on transport features which can be quantified from available data.
However, as said above, due to the transportation attribute of the dataset, there does not exist many situations for normal biases such as cultural biases over different kinds of social groups. While simultaneously, there was still a gap between what we can do and what we have. For instance, we can hardly confirm a thesis without preconceived notions. In the thesis statement and research question, we assumed pre-analysis that the richer district would be better connected, this gives a risk of us overestimating data that supports this hypothesis. But if just looking into the dataset, we cannot conclude which district has a greater economic advantage. Also, missing data of some certain time periods might cause deviation of results. All these defects may let our research simplify the complexities of real history and may lead readers, without the necessary background knowledge and archival support, to inevitably overlook the broader economic, political, and social factors that influenced Berlin's transportation during the Cold War.
